## Yahoo integration (Phase 3D)

`python -m app.browser_refresh` (or **Refresh Yahoo** in the Waiver Lab) runs one signed-in, read-only session in the dedicated Chrome profile. It captures starting rosters, all-player pages with status codes, my team page, the Can't Cut List, the "Available, IL Eligible" list and, after a period's first tipoff, daily team pages for current-week volumes, then re-captures rosters. All files are written in one atomic save with one `captured_at_utc`; nothing is written if the roster changes or a page fails.

Validation rejects the snapshot (no mixing) when its timestamp or player/roster fingerprints differ from the player capture, when any required section is missing (roster, adds, IL, waiver priority, Can't Cut, statuses, IL-eligible list), when team-page slots or IL use differ from the roster capture, when the add limit differs from the verified 4, or when listed players are not available. Actual state is rejected for missing pages, unverified headers or dates, or games in progress. Team IDs bind captures; names are mutable (Team 8 is now "Ball Don't Lie"), and the H2H CSV is matched by the names it was verified against.

Lock markers were not observable pre-tipoff; schedule tipoffs are applied as locks for all roster players.

Rows and plans carry `eligibility_class`: `verified_current` (verified snapshot under 15 minutes old, no gaps), `provisional` (feasible with named gaps such as stale evidence, unverified timing, or an out-status candidate), `conditional_future` (future-dated or waiver steps), `hold` or `infeasible`. ## Waiver Advisor (Phase 3E)

`GET /api/advisor?hashtag_weight=…&horizon=auto|1|2|3` calls `advise()` with the dashboard's cached forecast. Defaults live in `AdvisorConfig`; the page exposes only the horizon.

Why 3C.1 Strategies always returned HOLD on the October 20 capture: the retained-value rule had zero tolerance, so any final roster with less summed lineup value than today's failed (17 of 18 finalists were pruned before the rest-of-season check); the shortlist kept 6 adds chosen as one volume leader per counting stat with no matchup context, including an injured player (status O); and Streaming mode allowed only 2 droppable players. The 3B/3C weekly searches charged a fixed retention penalty that also favoured HOLD. Missing evidence set the budget to zero.

- **Horizon:** the current period plus the next (three periods when fewer than three days remain).
- **Pool:** every unowned FA/W player, minus missing or ambiguous projections, GP 0, Yahoo status O/NA, active claims and players with no horizon game.
- **Roster roles:** protected (Can't Cut; an IL player whose drop opens no active spot), core (rest-of-season lineup value more than 15% above the best available player at any of his positions), replaceable.
- **Search:** every eligible player is tried as a first move (now, each of his game days, and the day before a waiver game) against every replaceable player. The 12 best first-move adds plus 6 upgrade candidates feed a beam (width 8) of chronological multi-move plans within each period's add budget. A dropped player is never re-added; a pending waiver blocks earlier follow-ups; started players cannot be dropped after tipoff.
- **Objective:** horizon category points + 0.5 × horizon margin score + season term − 0.15 per add. The season term compares the final roster's rest-of-season margin score with HOLD: gains count at 0.5, losses beyond 0.5 count in full, smaller losses count as zero. The margin score is the existing `_smooth` logistic of margin over league spread; it is a ranking aid, not a probability. The deterministic rest-of-season flip count is shown beside it.
- **Fragility:** each shown flip is rechecked with Hashtag-only and LeagueWinner-only projections (opponents recomputed by the same engine) and a 5% margin shift. Each fragile flip subtracts 0.5 to form `rank_value`; plans need `rank_value` ≥ 0.25.
- **Distinct output:** best timing per transaction set, best drop per add set, then one card per idea (same dropped players and flips, or a subset/superset), with the rest listed as similar options. Up to five plus HOLD; if none qualify, the three closest alternatives with `why_not`.
- **Integrity:** the day engine picks starters by greedy lineup score with an augmenting-path check (exact for this slot structure) and falls back to `assign_lineup` on ties or locks. HOLD and opponent totals are asserted against the forecast and every shown plan is replayed through `_dated_team_totals`. Evidence gaps make plans provisional; they never block analysis (missing evidence assumes a full budget, marked `acquisition_count_unverified`).

The advisor scores 325 eligible players and returns four strategies: a Robert Williams III stream for Ajay Mitchell with Paul Reed for Jakob Poeltl and Christian Braun for Williams (+1 Week 2 category, REB fragile under Hashtag-only, stronger rest of season); a Williams-then-Cameron Johnson stream; Braun for Poeltl as a permanent upgrade; and Moussa Diabaté for Poeltl.

## Recommendation audit (Phase 3E.1)

Each shown strategy (at most five, never beam candidates) carries `audit`, rendered as the collapsed **Detailed audit** section of its card (see 3E.2).

- **Counterfactuals.** Every sub-plan of the recommended sequence is evaluated (all subsets up to five steps; above that, prefixes, singles, leave-one-outs and the net plan). Order and timing are kept. When an omitted step's player would have been dropped later, that later step drops the player he replaced instead (`rewire`), and the note says so. Each sub-plan is then checked with the advisor's own rules (roster membership, never re-adding a dropped player, protected/core players, per-period budgets, `drop_ok` locks); illegal ones are reported, not scored. Each scored sub-plan is replayed through `_dated_team_totals`.
- **Per transaction:** chronological gain over the previous prefix, leave-one-out contribution (full minus plan without it), the move made alone, and a verdict (`adds value`, `tradeoff`, `hurts`, `margins only`, `costs margin`, `no measurable value`, `required by a later move`). `interaction` is the full plan minus the sum of moves made alone; ≥0.5 category wins either way is flagged as non-additive.
- **Simpler plans.** Shorter legal sub-plans are `preferable` (short-term and rest-of-season category wins both no worse) or `comparable` (one within 0.5/1.0); the net-permanent plan is always shown for reference. A preferable one is named in the summary and warnings. Ranking is not changed.
- **Permanent changes.** The net plan pairs each final addition with the original player it replaces (temporary streams removed). Each pair shows per-game nine-category stats with attempts, GP, scheduled and expected remaining games, lineup starts (horizon under the plan, then post-horizon on the final roster; also after GP/82), lineup value, the swap alone, its share of this plan, pool scarcity, and flags: asset downgrade (lower rest-of-season lineup value), weaker per game with an edge resting on projected GP, short-term-only help, and a rest-of-season loss small enough for the ranking's 0.5 noise allowance to treat as free. Trade value is stated as not quantified.
- **Sources.** The plan, the net plan, simpler plans and leave-one-outs are re-scored with Hashtag-only and LeagueWinner-only (and Blended when another weight is selected). Each source rebuilds HOLD and all horizon and post-horizon opponent totals with the same engine. Flags: a short-term flip or a rest-of-season gain that disappears under one source, horizon margins under 5% of a typical weekly spread, and player-level source disagreement (GP ≥15 apart, per-game lineup value ≥25% apart, a counting stat ≥30% apart).
- **Integrity.** The audit asserts that its full-plan short-term wins, starts, rest-of-season wins and margin score equal the card's, that its Hashtag/LeagueWinner short-term deltas equal the card's `source_check`, and that horizon and rest-of-season periods do not overlap.

Rest-of-season review (no correctness bug found): the post-horizon set is the remaining non-actual periods after the horizon, so no period is counted twice. A dropped player is absent from every later week. Two behaviours can make a plan look better than it is, and the audit now surfaces both rather than changing ranking: the deterministic rest-of-season flip count swings on knife-edge categories (it can disagree in sign with the smoother margin score), and the ranking ignores rest-of-season margin losses under 0.5.

Runtime on the 22:39Z capture: advisor 11.8 s including 0.65 s of audit for four strategies (11.1 s before). `backend/run_advisor_snapshot.py [out.json]` runs the advisor on the saved live capture exactly as the dashboard does.

## Trade Lab (Phase 4A)

`GET /api/trades?hashtag_weight=…&partners=id,id&protect=id,id` calls `trade_lab()` with the dashboard's cached forecasts for the selected weight, Hashtag-only and LeagueWinner-only. Results are cached per weight, as-of, partners and protected set; `verification` is recomputed on every request so evidence age stays current. Defaults live in `TradeConfig`. Defaults: my team (Yahoo ID 4), all nine partners, no protected players, IL-slotted players left out. Nothing is proposed or sent to Yahoo.

- **Timing.** Trade end date, review type and reject time come from the verified settings capture (March 4, 2027; Commissioner; 2 days); analysis stops if they are missing or the deadline has passed. A trade is assumed to take effect `acceptance_days` (1) plus the reject window after the as-of date. Games before that day count for the original teams. The effective date, acceptance, review, pending trades, keeper value and injury returns are listed as unverified on every result. Yahoo's Can't Cut list limits drops, not trades, so it is not applied.
- **Two-team replay.** `_Context` builds one `_Engine` over all rostered players. Each team's rest-of-season weekly totals come from legal daily lineups with the old roster before the effective day and the new roster from it; current-week actual volumes are added as in the forecast. HOLD for all ten teams must reproduce `/api/deterministic` totals, records and seeds (assertion). For a trade, both teams are replayed, every matchup involving either is recomputed (including any meeting between them), and the full league is re-ranked with the forecast's tiebreak. Other teams keep their forecast totals.
- **Side metrics.** Rest-of-season category wins (W + T/2), the same with narrow flips counted at half (`adjusted_delta`; narrow = margin under 3% of the opponent total or 0.4 percentage points), per-category win changes, flips, next three weeks, record and seed before/after, lineup starts, incoming/outgoing starts, and league rank of each remaining-season category total.
- **Asset value.** Separate from fit. Per-game category z-scores over rostered non-IL players (shooting as makes above league average at the player's volume), summed, times GP/82, minus the best active, projectable free agent at the player's scarcest position, times team games after the effective day. `value_gap` is the difference as a share of the larger value (floor 25). Labels: Fair (±15%), Favours you / You pay a premium (to ±35%), Lopsided toward you / Overpay, or Both near replacement level. No market value or acceptance probability is produced.
- **Search.** All feasible pairs are screened with a linear estimate (outgoing player's baseline starts removed, incoming player's games times the start share of the nearest-scoring teammate). Pairs within ±60% value and a screen gain above −1 are ranked; the top 300 get the exact two-team replay. Up to 16 finalists (half reserved for mutual-looking trades) are re-run with each source alone.
- **Tiers.** Excluded: adjusted gain under 1.5; overpay beyond 35% unless the non-narrow gain is at least 3; lopsided toward me beyond 35% with partner gain under 0.5; partner losing more than 1.5, or losing anything without a value premium; or no gain for me under either source. Strong mutual fit: partner gain at least 0.5, value within ±20%, my gain positive and partner's non-negative under both sources. Everything else is a Speculative proposal. Only source-checked finalists can be shown. Cards: at most six, two per player I give, one per player I get. Rank score: my adjusted gain + half the partner's (clipped −2..3) − 3 × value gap beyond ±15% + 0.5 when my gain holds under both sources.
- **Page.** Fourth tab, loaded lazily. A context strip (no-trade baseline, earliest effective day, deadline), a comparison table with a No trade row, one card per trade (gain for each side, asset value, source check, categories gained/lost, why it could suit both teams, warnings, and a collapsed **Numbers** section), and a No trade baseline card. Partner choice and untouchable players sit under **Trade settings and search diagnostics**.

## Trade packages (Phase 4B)

`trade_lab()` now evaluates 1-for-1, 2-for-1 (you send two) and 1-for-2 (you receive two) packages with every partner (`TradeConfig.package_types`). The route, its cache key and every 1-for-1 number are unchanged; results add fields and report `phase: '4B'`. Cards keep `gives`/`receives` as each side's most valuable player and add `gives_all`, `receives_all`, `package`, `balancing`, `effective`, `one_for_one_subsets` and `simpler_alternative`.

- **Roster balancing.** Each team keeps at most 14 active players (10 starting slots plus 4 bench) and 3 IL. The side taking an extra player clears one spot by dropping an active player (never an incoming player, one of my untouchables, or a player on Yahoo's league-wide Can't Cut list when captured) or by moving an O/INJ player to a free IL slot (IL eligibility unverified; he is assumed not to return). The eight options with the least baseline lineup value are replayed in full (`drop_options=None` replays all) and the team keeps the one best for itself; the choice is rechecked once after the other side's fill, because a head-to-head meeting can change it. A side left with 13 active players is scored with the spot open and with each of the best current free agents (top 8 by rest-of-season lineup value plus the best at each base position). Waiver (W) players are never used. Trades themselves use no acquisitions.
- **Replacement-player opportunity cost.** A fill is used only when it beats the open spot and an acquisition is available in the effective week (adds left come from bound evidence; otherwise 4 are assumed and `acquisition_count_unverified` is flagged; partner adds are always unverified). The same free agent could be added without any trade, so ranking and tiers use `effective` = adjusted change − max(0, that free agent's value as a plain waiver swap for the team's cheapest legal player) − half of the best fill's edge over the next-best free agent. A package can be a Strong mutual fit only if both sides still clear their thresholds with the next-best free agent. A 2-for-1 whose fill adds at least 0.5 is flagged as freeing a useful streaming slot.
- **Trade value (fairness).** Superseded in 4D by blended market value and asymmetric labels (see Market-driven trades); kept here as history. The other manager is assumed to judge by draft position, not this model. `market_values()` uses Yahoo's preseason average draft position (standard scoring) from the public Draft Analysis page, captured read-only by `app/adp_capture.py` into `data/reports/yahoo-draft-analysis.json` (270 players, 187 drafted, captured October 21, 2026; the refresh recaptures it when older than seven days, and `python -m app.adp_capture` runs it alone). Value = 100 × (e^−(ADP−1)/40 − e^−(R−1)/40), R the rostered active players; undrafted players count as replacement (0). Without a capture it falls back to the mean of the two projection sources' ranks and `value.basis` says so. Yahoo's league player list is never used: from the first tipoff its default order is season-to-date stats (on October 21 it put Jokić at #245 and Kawhi Leonard at #644), which is why Myles Turner (ADP 108.8) for Ivica Zubac (59.0) + Julius Randle (68.5) briefly read as fair. `value.gap`/`value.label` are this trade value; the partner's side is what they receive minus any player they must cut. Packages above +60% toward you are not replayed; above +35% they are excluded as unlikely to be accepted; a Strong mutual fit needs trade value within ±20% and model value no worse than −20% for you. Model value stays as `value.model_gap`/`model_label` and drives your own overpay and low-usage guards. Cards show each player's ADP and warn when the two measures disagree. The pinned test archive holds a copy of the same ADP capture (preseason ADP is fixed once the season starts).
- **Model value.** A lone player keeps his 4A value. In a two-player side each player counts at no less than replacement level (0), since either could be swapped for a free agent. The gap is taken net of any player a side must drop.
- **New guards.** An outgoing player worth at least the best incoming player who starts under 60% of his remaining team games for me marks the package low-usage; it is excluded when I also pay a premium beyond 15%. Each multi-player finalist is replayed against its own 1-for-1 parts: a part within 0.5 of the package's effective gain, at no higher asset cost and no worse for the partner, becomes `simpler_alternative`, costs 1.5 rank points and is named on the card.
- **Search.** (1) Every unique package (sorted ID combinations), minus those needing two drops or with no legal drop. (2) Screen: value gap within ±60% and the 4A linear lineup estimate extended to several players, the cheapest drop and the top free agent. (3) Exact replay of the top 300 plausible 1-for-1s, the top 900 of each multi type and each partner's best 30 per type. (4) Team-week totals are cached by roster change, as are drop lists and waiver alternatives; essentially equivalent packages (same partner, one side identical, the other differing only by near-replacement players) are folded into the card they resemble. (5) 24 finalists (half for mutual-looking packages, four places per type) get drop costs and 1-for-1 comparisons and are re-run with Hashtag-only and LeagueWinner-only using the same balancing. Five cards are always shown: distinct qualifying packages, then qualifying alternatives that share a player (flagged `alternative`), then the closest source-checked packages labelled Below threshold with the reason (finalists are backfilled so this never needs an unchecked package; `qualifying_cards` gives the split). A player of mine appears in at most two cards; a received player appears once. `backend/run_trade_benchmark.py` measures runtime and coverage.
- **Controls.** One Trade search panel at the top holds trade partners (with All/None), trade types (1-for-1, 2-for-1, 1-for-2) and untouchable players, with a single Apply button. Changes show "Changes not applied yet"; Apply is disabled without a team or type. While searching, the button spins, an indeterminate bar and an elapsed-seconds line describe the scope, and earlier results dim (placeholder cards on the first run); stale responses are ignored. Results sit below; diagnostics and assumptions are collapsed at the bottom. `GET /api/trades` accepts `types=1-1,2-1,1-2` (default all; anything else is a 400) beside `partners` and `protect`, and caches up to 16 combinations.
- **Page.** Cards name the package type and show both sides' effective gains, a visible **Roster balancing** section (drop, IL move, open spot, fill with its waiver alternative, streaming slot) and, under **Numbers**, the replayed balancing options, the 1-for-1 parts and folded equivalents. Diagnostics give generated/plausible/replayed/finalist/shown counts by type.

## Search completeness (October 21, 2026)

Both searches now aim at the best viable options from the complete option set within about 1.5 minutes, using forked worker processes (`workers`, default min(8, cores − 1); one process when fork is unavailable or the job is small). Workers inherit the prepared engine copy-on-write and return only scores, so results are identical to a single-process run (checked: all 3,726 Trade Lab replays matched exactly).

- **Trade Lab.** Every unique package (24,696 with all nine partners) passes the hard roster checks and the cheap screen. Every viable package is then replayed exactly (`replay_all_viable`): one the partner would not see as lopsided by ADP (trade value gap ≤ +35%, the exclusion line) and where you do not give up more than 60% in model value (`viable_model_floor`). There is no screen-score cut and no per-type quota; on the pinned capture that is 3,726 packages (263 1-for-1, 1,709 2-for-1, 1,754 1-for-2). Every legal drop is replayed (`drop_options=None`; the previous eight-cheapest rule picked a worse drop for 86 of them). Free-agent fills still try the top 8 by lineup value plus the best at each position. Replay takes 36 s with eight workers (115 s in one process); the whole lab about 45 s plus about 22 s for cold forecasts. Non-viable packages cannot reach a card, so nothing that could be recommended is skipped.
- **Waiver Advisor.** Stage 1 scores every first move (every eligible add, action day and legal drop), then a beam of 32 states over the 40 best adds plus 20 permanent upgrades (previously 8, 12 and 6). Stage 2 is local improvement: the 8 best distinct plans try every single change (another add or day for one move, another drop, removing a move, inserting a move, over 60+ candidate adds) and keep the best improvement; converged plans are replaced by the next-best unexplored ones until a fixed budget of 300,000 evaluations is spent (deterministic, so repeated runs match). The search and the 400-idea description cut now use the same score as the final list: value minus the fragile-flip discount, with fragility (a flip one source alone or a 5% margin shift reverses) computed for every evaluated plan. On the October 21 live capture the old default found 1.39 in 14 s and the old widest beam (96 states, 120 adds) 1.95 in 102 s; the new default finds 2.04 in 84 s, and a five-minute run with twice the width and 1.6 million evaluations finds the same top two plans (#3 differs by 0.009).

- **2-for-2 packages (October 21, 2026).** `package_types` now includes `2-2` (a fourth trade-type toggle; `types=…,2-2` on the route). Roster sizes do not change, so there is no balancing; each finalist is compared with its four 1-for-1 parts. Generation reaches 99,225 feasible packages (74,529 of them 2-for-2). Fairness checks run first and the lineup screen runs only on the 21,317 viable packages, in parallel (35 s → 2.7 s); every viable package is replayed exactly. ## Market-driven trades (Phase 4D)

The Trade Lab serves only my team. It looks for trades that give a partner players the market values highly in exchange for players who do more for my nine categories. Giving away market value is allowed and is ranked by what it buys. Fairness is no longer the main decision. Nothing predicts acceptance.

- **Market value (`market_values`, `market_summary`).** Two independent public signals. (1) Yahoo preseason ADP, standard scoring (existing `yahoo-draft-analysis.json`). (2) FantasyPros expert consensus rankings, Roto/categories (`data/reports/fantasypros-ecr.json`, captured read-only by `app.adp_capture.capture_ecr` with a plain GET; `python -m app.adp_capture --ecr-only`; the Yahoo refresh recaptures it when three days old). Each signal is re-ranked to an ordinal position among Yahoo's players (ties averaged), mapped to the existing curve 100 × (e^−(pos−1)/40 − e^−(R−1)/40), and the two are averaged. A side's market value counts its best player in full and each further player at `depth_discount` (0.5): the side that gets the best player in a deal is the one managers see as winning it. Without it, plain sums called Kawhi Leonard + Chet Holmgren (98) even with Nikola Jokić + Yaxel Lendeborg (98); with it the partner would lose about 23%. Undrafted players count as replacement by ADP (an observation). A drafted player FantasyPros does not list counts as missing for ECR, not zero. ECR older than 14 days at the as-of time drops out. With neither signal the projection-source ranks are used and `basis` says so. Matching uses the normalized name, the team for duplicates, and first initial + surname + team for names Yahoo abbreviates ("N. Alexander-Walker"); 6 of 540 ECR rows are unmatched, none in the top 200. On October 21 both signals cover all 140 rostered active players; one disagrees materially (Paolo Banchero: ADP value 38, ECR 19). FantasyPros has not published in-season ROS rankings yet: its ROS page serves the final draft consensus (6 experts, updated October 20), recorded as `ranking_type: Draft`, `fallback_for: ROS`. Yahoo's league player list and Yahoo's own rank column are not used. My model value is never used as market perception.
- **Package fields.** `value.market` holds `give`, `get`, `my_cut` and `partner_cut` (market value of a player either side must drop), `partner_surplus` = give − get − partner_cut, `my_cost` = give + my_cut − get, `by_signal` gaps and `signals_disagree` (signal gaps more than 25 points apart). `value.gap` keeps its meaning (positive favours me by market value). The z-score model value stays as `model_gap` and is shown only as an asset-sacrifice risk.
- **Competitive benefit.** Unchanged two-team replay. `_robust` = the lower of the blended effective gain and each source's gain alone (net of any fill opportunity cost). Cards show rest-of-season and next-three-week change, categories gained and lost, daily starts both ways, seed change, source checks, and standings-rival effects (partner within two seeds).
- **Tiers.** `excluded`: effective gain below 1.5; gain disappears under both sources; partner market loss above `viable_market_limit` (10%; no manager would consider a deal that loses them value, so such packages are never shown); or market cost above `expensive_cost_per_win` (12 market points per robust win) when the gain is under 3.0, half or more of it rests on narrow flips, or a source shows no gain. Otherwise `recommended`. Partner category loss, low usage and model-value overpay are risks, not exclusions.
- **Labels (one per card, in this order).** Expensive improvement: market cost over 10 points and over 12 per robust win. Mutually beneficial: partner gains at least 0.5, not fill-dependent, no source shows a partner loss. Market opportunity: partner gains more than 15% by market value. Market-balanced: otherwise (partner loses at most 10% or gains at most 15%).
- **Rank score (`ranking` parts, in category wins).** Robust gain; plausibility −3 × gap when the partner loses market value; market cost −0.02 per point surrendered; next three weeks +0.25 per win (±2 cap); sources +0.5 when both hold, −0.5 when split; partner +0.25 per win (−2 to +3 cap); complexity −0.25 per extra player and per drop, IL move or fill; simpler 1-for-1 −1.5. All weights are declared heuristics.
- **Pareto pruning.** Before finalists, a package is dropped when another with the same partner and at least one shared player is no worse on my gain, market cost, plausibility and package size, within 0.25 on the next three weeks, and better on one (`dominated_by`, `dominated_variations`).
- **Search.** A package is replayed when the partner's estimated market loss (cheapest cut assumed) is at most 10% and either the model-value give-up is within 60% or, past that, the linear screen gives me at least 0.5 (`floor_screen_gate`). Paying a premium never removes a package. Finalists: up to four partner-premium packages, four places per type, then the best remaining (24 in all). Cards: up to five recommended. `keep_finalists=True` also returns every source-checked finalist (tests and diagnostics; the page does not set it). Cards are never padded with below-threshold packages, and there is no section for trades the partner would lose on (an earlier 4D draft had one; it was removed).
- **Page.** Comparison columns You, Them, Market to them, Sources, Label. Each card shows the label, a Market value tile, and sections My projected gain, What I give up, What they receive, Why they would consider it, Why I benefit, Risks. The Numbers table adds ECR and market value. Diagnostics list market sources, coverage and freshness.
